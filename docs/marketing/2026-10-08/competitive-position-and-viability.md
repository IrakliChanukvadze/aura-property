# What makes the three plans viable?

Overnight pass 1, 8 October 2026. Proposals and arithmetic using the existing illustrative prices/costs; no private financial figures, purchases or market commitments.

## New competitive evidence: free is already available

Korter's own developer page states that listing a building/cottage project is free and describes promotion on its platform and external sources. Therefore **free listings plus Premium promotion are not a unique business model**. This does not invalidate Pini's direction, but it changes the reason a developer or buyer should choose it. [Korter developer information](https://korter.ge/en/message), checked 8 October 2026.

Korter also publishes a roughly six-month property-selection claim, but does not expose a sample/methodology on the inspected page. Do not adopt it as Pini's sales-cycle forecast or proof of attribution.

### Differentiation hypotheses worth testing

| Candidate advantage | Observable promise | Test before claiming it |
|---|---|---|
| Easier map-to-choice | A new visitor can reach a relevant project, understand current information and find the correct recipient | Moderated same-task comparison; record errors/time and comprehension, not preference alone |
| More dependable current information | Visible source/update date and explicit unknowns, with a defined refresh process | Inspect a sampled cohort for completeness, freshness and recipient accuracy |
| Easier developer onboarding | Accurate record assembled with a predictable, low-friction process | Time from valid submission to publish, missing-field loops and actual staff minutes |
| Better category separation | Projects, houses and land show appropriate facts; no standalone flats clutter the project offering | Ask users to explain category differences and find each without accidentally entering the wrong funnel |
| Better local storytelling | Original observations answer practical questions around actual map pins | Compare relevant destination visits and qualified connections, not copied-video views |

Do not claim Pini has achieved these advantages before collecting evidence. The first public message should describe the product's actual experience; “simpler” or “more current” becomes defensible through repeated performance.

## Contribution requirements, not revenue excitement

The current plan envelopes are exploratory investment. Revenue arithmetic cannot demonstrate that the same budget will be recovered. Count variable labor, rework, refunds and support before using contribution to pay for acquisition and overhead.

### Plan A: promotion subscription

Existing hypothesis: $149/month price, $60 direct service cost, $89 contribution. The $6,000 three-month envelope averages $2,000/month. Covering that envelope alone would need **23 active paying accounts** ($2,047 contribution/month), assuming costs stay at $60 and all accounts pay for the full period. It does not cover additional platform overhead outside the envelope.

With ten accounts, monthly contribution is only $890. With 20% monthly churn as a deliberately uncertain example, 23 accounts need about five new accounts/month just to replace departures. At $120 acquisition cost, that replacement alone uses about $600/month; any acquisition spending already inside the envelope must not be counted twice.

Implication: do not sell a labor-heavy monthly package at $149 while assuming ten developers fund a full brand team. Simplify the package, raise willingness-to-pay through proven value, or treat it as a limited validation pilot rather than profitability.

### Plan B: buyer assistance and agency economics

Existing buyer-service hypothesis: $99 revenue minus $50 direct labor/admin gives $49 contribution. Covering the $9,000 three-month envelope from this fee alone needs **184 bookings over 90 days**, about 61/month, before acquisition/refunds/extra overhead. A five- or ten-customer pilot proves service usefulness, not that this fee supports the strategy.

Existing agency illustration: $1,500 contribution per sale. Six completed sales would mathematically cover $9,000, but the sale timing, actual fee, fulfillment costs and attribution are unverified. At 10% qualified-to-sale probability, six expected sales require 60 qualified conversations; expected values are not guaranteed transactions. No day-90 sale requirement should encourage falsely marking pipeline as revenue.

Implication: keep Aura's actual agency economics separate from Pini's proposed fee. If Aura already provides the same guidance through representation, a Pini concierge charge needs a clear additional benefit.

### Plan C: production packages

Existing $900 package hypothesis: $600 direct cost, $300 contribution. The $5,500 three-month envelope averages $1,833/month; covering it requires **seven packages/month** ($2,100 contribution), before overhead outside the envelope. Three packages contribute only $900/month.

Capacity example, not a quote: if one package consumes eight production/review hours, seven consume 56 hours/month before sales, project-data updates or brand content. If average rework rises by $300/package, the contribution becomes zero. Production consistency and fixed revision scope therefore matter more than a low advertised model price.

The later scoped model in [production-packages.md](production-packages.md) supersedes these coarse cost/capacity assumptions for the three-master/seven-variant offer: $714.21 direct cost leaves $185.79 contribution and requires **ten packages/month** to cover $1,833/month. At 22 fulfillment hours/package this needs 220 hours/month, while a 100-hour production allocation supports only four complete packages. The current $900 scope, capacity and budget therefore do not fit together; revise price, scope or staffing before choosing Plan C. These remain planning assumptions, not observed costs or demand.

## Pricing pilot design

1. Choose one customer and exact problem; do not interview “everyone” about an undefined Premium.
2. Define a small deliverable, rights, duration, revision count and what remains free.
3. Present a real price hypothesis and ask what expense/work it replaces.
4. After outreach and commercial approval, prefer an actual paid pilot to hypothetical survey enthusiasm.
5. Track direct hours, accepted output, customer acceptance, refunds and renewal/repeat intent.
6. Review whether customers buy the scope or require costly exceptions. Adjust price/scope before scaling.

## Map-first can coexist with search visibility

Keep the map as the primary interaction while exposing crawlable text links to useful category/city/detail pages. A pin or search box alone is not a dependable discovery path for Google; official guidance favors real anchor links and reachable content. This is a supporting navigation/SEO architecture, not a proposal to replace the map with a list-first site. [Google link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable), [site structure](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/help-google-understand-your-ecommerce-site-structure?hl=en).

Audit existing Pini localized routes/sitemap before adding new routes. Do not generate hundreds of empty city/category SEO pages. Useful Projects/Houses/Land category pages must represent real content, and project apartments remain nested rather than becoming a standalone flat feed.

## Decision consequence

Plan A remains the recommended discovery foundation, but its acquisition message needs a concrete advantage beyond “free.” Aura can pursue Plan B independently. Plan C is worth testing only if real customer prices and reliable accepted-output costs support contribution. Choose the first pilot based on actual customer pain, supply and operator capacity—not the largest hypothetical revenue number.
